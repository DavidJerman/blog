I am writing this post almost a year later. What brought me back to these notes is the fact that I am planning another trip to Japan and beyond, yet again. I thought to myself, why not go over the photos I took and finally write a bit more about it...

Karuizawa was a unique place, completely unlike anywhere else I had visited in Japan. It felt as though I had stepped into a European village that just happened to be full of Japanese people. I am exaggerating, of course, but Karuizawa does have a lot of European-style buildings, and even some Western (though perhaps not *that* authentic) restaurants sporting German names. 

One of these restaurants was *Kitzbuhl*, clearly borrowing its name from the Austrian town of Kitzbühel. The restaurant had a bunch of signs outside saying *Delikatessen*, *Restaurant*, and *Kaffee*. Japanese often romanticize Europe just like Europeans like me often romanticize Japan and other places abroad. So I am sure many of them were eager to try the sausages and *Leberkäse* (a type of traditional baked meatloaf, I couldn't think of a proper translation at first!). I personally did not try any of this, as I was not after European food that day. Though I have to admit, the thought of getting some pizza or a dish close to home did cross my mind here and there!

![The Kitzbuhl restaurant](/blog/media/travel/karuizawa/kitzbuhl_restaurant.jpg)

Another interesting building I saw was a Christian church. And not just one. Looking at Google Maps, there are at least three of them in Karuizawa. This is very unusual, since Japan is primarily a Buddhist and Shinto country. But it was not out of place at all, because the surrounding houses looked remarkably like what I am used to seeing back home, especially if you visit countryside villages with holiday homes partly made of wood. A lot of residences in Karuizawa are also meant as summer getaways, so it makes total sense that it gives off a similar vibe.

![The church](/blog/media/travel/karuizawa/karuizawa_church.jpg)

Now, one might ask where this European influence comes from... history time!

Back in the late 1800s, Karuizawa was actually just a quiet, fading post town along the old Nakasendo highway. Everything changed in 1886 when a Canadian missionary named Alexander Croft Shaw stumbled upon it. He was looking for a way to escape the brutal summer heat of Tokyo and fell in love with the cool mountain climate, which reminded him of home. 

Shaw built a summer villa and a chapel here, calling the town a "hospital without a roof." It didn't take long for other Western diplomats, missionaries, and traders to follow his lead and start building their own alpine retreats. This completely shaped the town's architectural style and explains why you suddenly find multiple historic Christian churches hidden in the woods here. 

By the early 1900s, the Japanese elite and even the Imperial family noticed the trend and started building their own summer villas right alongside them. Over the decades, it grew into the high-end, unique mountain resort it is today - a place where you can find a piece of the European Alps nestled right inside the Japanese mountains.

The first day I spent mainly just exploring the town and observing the unusual architecture. But the next day, after a hearty breakfast at a semi-hotel, I decided I would visit the famous Karuizawa waterfalls. For that, I had to take a bus. The problem was, it was absolutely packed with tourists... So full, in fact, that on the way back I had to wait for a second bus, AND I ended up sitting on one of those sketchy extendable fold-out seats right in the middle of the aisle.

![The food](/blog/media/travel/karuizawa/food_in_karuizawa.jpg)

Still, getting out into nature after being in the town felt really refreshing. I suppose I might have set my expectations a bit too high, though, since the waterfalls themselves were a little underwhelming and there wasn't much else to do around there. So, I just caught the bus back and spent the rest of the day continuing my exploration of the town.

![The waterfall](/blog/media/travel/karuizawa/waterfall.jpg)

And that was honestly it for Karuizawa - a short stop in a town that completely defies the standard formula of what you expect to find in Japan.
